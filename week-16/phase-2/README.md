# Phase 2 — Remediation, Verification and Final Readout

## Overview

Phase 2 built on the initial assessment. The team reviewed and prioritised the assessment work, supported remediation activities, considered the available verification evidence and prepared the final outputs for technical and business stakeholders.

The work was part of a simulated pre-investment security and AI-risk assessment. This public summary describes the work at a high level and does not disclose confidential engagement information.

## What the team did

- Reviewed and prioritised security and AI-risk improvement areas according to potential business impact.
- Discussed practical remediation options and supported changes through a controlled, reviewable workflow.
- Considered test results, peer review and available verification evidence.
- Identified items that still required confirmation in the deployed environment.
- Prepared technical findings and an executive-level summary.
- Developed a practical 30/60/90-day security and AI-governance roadmap.
- Prepared and delivered a final readout explaining the overall assessment, priorities, limitations and recommended next steps.

## My contribution — Cloud and Infrastructure Security

I continued in my assigned Cloud and Infrastructure Security workstream. I contributed to an infrastructure remediation change intended to narrow service access to the approved application load-balancer security group instead of broad public ingress. I participated in the related remediation discussions and reviewed the available change and test evidence.

I also supported wider team testing and verification, evidence tracking, reporting and roadmap discussions. I was careful to distinguish between a change prepared in the code, a result tested in the available environment and a change confirmed as deployed. The production configuration was not independently verified, so I did not claim a confirmed production remediation.

## What I learned

Phase 2 strengthened my understanding that remediation must address the underlying risk and be supported by review and testing. It also developed my ability to communicate technical work in business terms, contribute across workstreams and be transparent about verification limits.

A security report is most useful when it does more than list technical issues: it explains why they matter, what should happen first, who needs to act and how progress can be verified.
